## Why

The planner loses receiver destinations after declaration and rejects useful passing behind fixed action tiers. Broad self-play cannot establish useful skill use until the shared AI can finish scoring plans, handle the last two Sevens turns, and protect possession and players when scoring is out of reach.

## What changes

- Preserve concrete activation targets through commands and search trials, using the same coach in matches and experiments.
- Evaluate passing skills, shared reroll resources, late-half scoring runs and receiver preparation. Permit a risky scoring plan when safer progress cannot produce a touchdown.
- Adapt defence and player preservation to actual opponents, remaining players, time and persistent league injuries. Keep Bloodlust and other capability planning in the shared core.
- Add a resumable policy lab which runs tactical gates before fresh, balanced full-match acquisition on Daily Driver. Preserve exact sources, replay checks, action decisions and reasons.
- Give all skill and trait entries an explicit evidence contract, separating AI-selected opportunities, mechanical rules and lifecycle exclusions. Publish a dark visual progress and results page.

## Capabilities

### New capabilities

- `turn-planning`: Retained tactical intent, skill-aware transfer decisions, late-half scoring and context-aware defensive preservation.
- `policy-evaluation`: Repeatable tactical gates, opportunity records, frozen opponent comparisons and fair Sevens evidence.

### Modified capabilities

None.

## Impact

Shared simulation AI, focused behavior tests, coach scripts and OpenSpec artifacts. Builds on the pending skill-plan milestone without changing its frozen evidence. Compute runs on Daily Driver through the existing SSH route. Generated data stays outside source control. No paid model calls in the playing policy and no new runtime dependencies.
